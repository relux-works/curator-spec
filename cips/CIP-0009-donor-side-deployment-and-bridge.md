## Revision 3: the owner decisions of 2026-10-10

- **Three deployment variants (D0).** A (qualified lockdown under the donor user, no administrator rights) is the first milestone; B (separate harness and bridge OS identities, one administrator request, no Keychain) follows once its separate donor provisioner is specified and qualified; C (sandbox under the donor user) is the fallback when administrator rights are declined or unavailable. D1 and D2 describe B; D0 scopes A and C.
- **First contact without a human second channel (D4, D6).** Web PKI is required. DNSSEC validated inside the client is the default, with DoH as a transport fallback. Only an environment explicitly declared to lack a DNSSEC domain uses invitation pins instead. When both are present they must agree; a read or validation failure never selects the invitation fallback. Records are per environment; the donor configures nothing.
- **Strict harness admission** for every variant: CIP-0008 R9 (the admission rule, exact qualified builds checked before exec, the bridge refusing any session that is not `lockdown` + `remote-auto`).
- **Join point and `init`** are specified (D9, D10); the carrier phases are decided in D11 and decision 5.

## Revision 2: what the architecture review changed

- **F18:** the project bridge never signs as the donor-held worker key: the **donor-side bridge signs the final envelope bytes** with the worker's signing key; our side verifies, admits and carries the bytes unchanged under a distinct, audited carrier authority (D5).

## Summary

This CIP specifies the **donor side** of a remote worker and the **bridge** between the donor's machine and the project's host: what Curator installs on the donor machine at first deployment (in one of three variants: the lockdown posture only, two OS identities with a kernel sandbox and a per-UID egress policy, or a kernel sandbox under the donor's own user; plus a locked configuration, a trusted bridge service and a minimal remote-tools relay), how the initial exchange works (invitation, three role-separated keys, a signed enrolment transcript over a server challenge, join point, root-signed admission, an authenticated-encrypted credential package), how the harness talks to our host (one SSH connection from the donor's bridge identity towards our host, carrying the framed `swarma-rb/1` protocol and the mailbox), how MITM and replay are defended (Web PKI with locally validated DNSSEC by default or invitation pins for a declared no-DNSSEC environment, host/user CAs, signed transcripts and envelopes over exact bytes), and how a worker is stopped, retired, revoked or purged on both sides. It decides that the remote tools are an **MCP relay** while the initial exchange is a **Curator CLI step**, never a tool the model can call. Diagrams: [first deployment](diagrams/donor-first-deployment.puml), [join handshake](diagrams/join-handshake.puml), [remote turn](diagrams/remote-worker-turn.puml), [retirement](diagrams/remote-worker-retire.puml), [C4 containers](diagrams/donor-side.dsl) and its [PlantUML component view](diagrams/donor-side.puml), [deployment variants](diagrams/donor-variants.puml), [independent lifecycle axes](diagrams/donor-lifecycle.puml), and [state layout](diagrams/donor-layout.puml).

## Motivation and user stories

- **Owner (2026-10-08):** "Curator on the donor machine manages the MCP setup, including a local MCP server for the remote tools, at first deployment of the environment"; "what the donor sees, how they stop it, what is logged on their side"; "the full set: handshake, join point, DNS fingerprints, MITM, Remote Bash, mailbox, retire, with diagrams".
- **A person who downloaded our toolchain** and wants to run their agent on our task: one invitation, one command, a visible result ("registered, waiting for tasks"), and a way out that does not depend on us.
- **Our orchestrator** wants the remote worker to be an ordinary worker: pin-checked launch, mailbox tasks, results as outcome proposals, no commands ever sent towards the donor's machine (architecture §7.9: connection towards the host only, C19).
- **Both owners** want one strong boundary and few moving parts (remote-workplace design rule). The review showed that the strongest donor boundary needs two OS identities, which need administrator rights once; the owner does not want a donor asked for those rights by default (2026-10-10). Hence three deployment variants (D0).

## Current state

- `rwh` (relux-works/remote-worker-harness) runs Claude, Codex and Muse on the dedicated Mac mini with the built-ins locked (CIP-0008 §Current state) and the tools served from the project machine over `ssh -R` (a reverse-forwarded Unix socket) or through a relay; its `rwh worker` mode turns addressed a2a messages into turns under allow-from, budget and rate guards; for Muse it already measured a **stdio relay over a Unix socket** with the Seatbelt profile. The tunnel is opened **by the project side into the remote machine** and the remote machine is *ours*; nothing exists for a foreign donor, and the host side keeps no per-worker OS user (that is the remote workplace of architecture §7.9, designed in VISION v1.3 and not yet implemented). `rwh`'s Codex path is a single auth owner with an explicit lock and external access tokens for tenants (CIP-0010 C2).
- relux-works/remote-workplace v2.1 (RWP) gives a **container** host side of tool execution: a fresh hardened container per call inside a dedicated VM, a per-worker policy (tools, write scope, network mode, limits), change extraction gates adopted by R-TRANSFER (D5a), an egress service, audit and cleanup. Its guarantees belong to that path (RWP §1, §9) and are not inherited by an OS-user executor whose tool names look alike. VISION v1.3 §0 and the owners' call (C9) choose an **OS-user workplace** on our host for the first implementation, while the architecture diagram `remote-worker-start` still depicts the container path: a source reconciliation the owners should settle when adopting this CIP.
- Curator today: no `remote-worker` commands; MCP declaration packages are `git`-sourced `agent-mcp.json` with `stdio` (bare command on `PATH`) or `http` (`https` only) transports, bounded by `mcp_package_allowlist` entries that are **canonical source identities** (§2.2); the manager never installs or launches a server (§2.2); first-party modules have roots (Decision 0009); CIP-0007 proposes operator-trusted provisioning of binaries; the umbrella provider at revision A warns rather than refuses on a provider outside trusted roots.
- Keys: the key keeper of architecture §7.2 is design; `curator-trust` is a v0.4 draft; today's a2a uses per-agent Matrix accounts with tokens in `~/.config/a2a-swarm/` (0600).

## Design

### Options considered

**A. Where the remote tools live**
1. *MCP server on our host, reached by the harness over `http`* (environments §2.2 `http`). The harness would need a network path and an authentication secret it holds itself. Rejected.
2. *One local MCP server in the harness's OS user, holding the SSH client and the keys* (revision 1). The review showed it defeats the separation the design claims: a shared UID cannot be told apart by a per-UID firewall, the shared home exposes audit, state and (on the file fallback) the private key, and a keyring item is not a non-exportable signing service. Rejected as a claim of OS isolation; A explicitly accepts a shared UID with qualified lockdown only.
3. *A minimal stdio **relay** plus a trusted **bridge service**, joined by a local socket; separate identities and peer-UID admission in B, a sandbox in C, qualified lockdown only in A.* The harness sees a local process with no keys and no network; the bridge holds everything that must not be reached. `rwh` measured the relay shape for Muse. **Recommended.**
4. *Curator skill with scripts.* Scripts need a local shell, which the lockdown removes. Rejected for tools; kept for prompts.

**B. How the donor receives tasks and messages**
1. *A carrier client (Matrix) on the donor machine.* Mirrors our bridge, but puts carrier credentials on a foreign machine and adds a second network path. Later option.
2. *Mailbox over the tool channel.* The same SSH connection carries the mailbox control frames; the worker's **carrier** account lives in **our** bridge (C2 of the owners' call: the agent never reads the carrier), while the worker's **signing** key stays on the donor and signs every outbound envelope (F18). The donor holds no carrier credential at all. **Recommended for the first release.**

**C. The initial exchange**
1. *An MCP tool `enrol`.* The model would see the key material or at least drive the exchange. Rejected.
2. *A Curator CLI step run by a human on the donor machine* (`curator remote-worker init <invitation>`), with keys generated by the bridge role and D0 variant-specific storage; UID/sandbox isolation in B/C, no OS confidentiality guarantee in A. **Recommended.**

**D. Trust bootstrap for the first contact**
1. *DNS-published trust with client-side DNSSEC validation.* Chosen on 2026-10-10 as the default: the embedded root trust anchor, DoH transport fallback, and Web PKI, with invitation pins cross-checked when present. Validation errors and mismatches refuse.
2. *Invitation pins for an environment declared to lack a DNSSEC domain.* Chosen as the fallback on 2026-10-10; Web PKI still applies. The declaration is a trust-mode choice before lookup, not a response to a DNS failure. No human fingerprint comparison or second channel is required.

**D0. Deployment variants** (owner decision 2026-10-10). `init` records the variant in the enrolment and in the qualified tuple; the project's workplace policy may require a minimum variant (for example write access only for B or C), and `status` and the session banner name it.

Lockdown only** | none | the CIP-0008 lockdown posture with strict qualification (R9): the model is offered no local tool and only an exactly qualified harness build starts; no OS boundary beyond the donor's user | a user service of the donor (macOS LaunchAgent, Linux `systemd --user`), running while the donor is logged in | non-exportable Secure Enclave keys on Macs that have one (owner decision 2026-10-10; P-256 signing and SSH keys); 0600 files in the donor's state directory on Linux, TPM later | first milestone |
| **B. Two identities** | once, at `init` | D1 and D2: an untrusted harness identity and a trusted bridge identity, per-UID egress, the kernel read boundary, a root-owned executable root | the bridge identity, as a system service that survives logout | 0600 files owned by the bridge identity, **no Keychain** (owner decision 2026-10-10); non-exportable keys later only if the Secure Enclave proves usable from such a service without Keychain | the most reliable; disabled until the separate donor provisioner is specified and qualified |
| **C. Sandbox** | none | everything under the donor's user; the harness inside the OS sandbox (macOS Seatbelt; Linux Landlock, or bubblewrap with a private network namespace): it reads and writes only its managed home and scratch directory, connects only to the relay socket and the local provider proxy, and reaches no Keychain or other IPC service; the bridge's state is outside its readable/writable set; pinned runtime executables are admitted read/execute only, never writable | a user service of the donor | as in A | the fallback when the rights are not granted or cannot be |

- **A states its limit.** If a qualified harness build turned out to offer a local tool after all, the model would act as the donor's user. That is the risk accepted for the first milestone; exact builds checked before exec and the bridge's posture check (CIP-0008 R9) are its guards, and the qualification lab of CIP-0008 R7 is what keeps a build out.
- **The lock without a system layer.** In A and C the locked configuration lives in the donor's own files. A offers no local tool to the model but makes no OS integrity guarantee against a compromised harness process; C keeps the file outside its writable set; the donor can, and then the bridge stops the worker instead of running it open (CIP-0008 R9).
- **Executables.** In A and C they live in the donor's state directory (`~/Library/Application Support/swarma/<release>/` on macOS, `~/.local/share/swarma/<release>/` on Linux) and the bridge verifies each digest before exec; in B they live in the root-owned `/opt/swarma/releases/<release>/`.
- **Egress (R-NET-TOOL).** By default the donor loop reaches only its model-provider endpoints and our host; no web/search/fetch tool or donor-side web server runs. B uses per-UID firewall rules; C uses the sandbox's network rules (connect only to the provider proxy and the relay socket); A has no OS egress control of its own, and the harness reaches its provider directly. This donor limitation is explicit; every project-side workplace has zero egress in every donor variant. D5b specifies pending trusted-circle web options.
- **Layout.** The donor side follows the layout of the central environment. In B the root is `/opt/swarma`: `libexec/`, `etc/`, `lib/`, `run/`, `services/swarma-bridge-<project>/` (the bridge identity's home), `workers/worker-<project>/` (the harness identity's home) and `releases/<release>/` (pinned executables). In A and C the same subdirectories sit under the donor's state root.
- **Selection.** `init` asks for administrator rights only when the donor chooses B. Once B and C are qualified on a platform, `init` offers B and falls back to C; whether A then stays selectable is open question 7.

In B, everything the first draft put "in the same user, protected by the keyring's access control" is in the bridge identity. The harness identity cannot read the bridge's home, cannot connect to anything but the relay socket and the proxy, and cannot alter any control state; this is true by UID and sandbox, not by promise.

**D2. OS sandbox and provisioning (F11, F14), variants B and C.** Variant C applies the sandbox bullet below without the accounts, the per-UID egress and the root-owned executable root; variant A applies none of this section. One platform is qualified first: **macOS** (the platform `rwh` measured). Linux follows with its own qualification; Windows is out of scope (owners' call C18). Unsupported hosts refuse at `init`.
- *Accounts:* two hidden accounts without login shell or password, created by the separately specified donor provisioner with allocated UIDs (B disabled while pending); the bridge account's home holds the keys (0600 files owned by the bridge identity in the first release; non-exportable keys are later hardening only if usable without Keychain; B does not use Keychain) and the state; the harness account's home holds the managed home and the scratch dir.
- *Service domain:* the bridge service is a `launchd` system daemon running as the bridge identity, so it starts at boot, survives logout and does not need a user session; the harness is launched by the bridge service as the harness identity.
- *Egress:* a `pf` anchor with per-UID rules (harness → loopback proxy port only; bridge → our SSH host and join point only; everything else blocked both ways). Because the identities are distinct, UID rules are meaningful.
- *Sandbox:* a Seatbelt profile applied to the harness process with an explicit allow-list: read and execute of the pinned executable root and its runtime libraries, read and write of the managed home and the scratch dir, connect to the relay socket and the proxy port; deny everything else (file reads, AppleEvents, pasteboard, LaunchServices `open`, other sockets). The **read boundary is mandatory**, not "where available"; it is what makes Muse's retained read tools harmless and what keeps the bridge's home unreadable even if the file permissions were wrong. The profile revision is part of the qualified tuple (CIP-0008 R3) and of the plan (R5).
- *Protected executable root:* `/opt/swarma/releases/<release>/` owned by root, read-only, no writable predecessor directory; the bridge launches every binary (harness, relay, proxy, itself) by absolute path from it and verifies the digest against the pinned release manifest first (D3).
- *Transactional provisioning:* the separate donor provisioner must journal each step (accounts, homes, egress anchor, sandbox profile, daemon unit) and undo a failed provisioning in reverse order; a half-provisioned machine never keeps a relaxed rule; `init` reports the exact step that failed.
- *Linux (second platform):* two system users with `nologin`; `nftables` with `meta skuid`; the bridge as a `systemd` system service; the harness started through `systemd-run --uid` with `NoNewPrivileges`, `PrivateTmp`, `RestrictAddressFamilies`, a cgroup for lifetime ownership, and **Landlock** as the read boundary (ProtectSystem is a write boundary, not a read-confidentiality one); the managed home mounted writable, the executable root read-only. Qualified separately before any Linux donor.
- *Negative tests per platform* at the real `init`, launch, tool and stop entries: reads outside the allow-list refused, egress outside the allowlist refused, the proxy refuses a non-provider host, in B the relay socket refuses a connection from another UID, the separately specified donor provisioner refuses a colliding label, a symlink in a path argument, arbitrary-account removal and an injected argument; provisioning interrupted halfway rolls back; a pre-existing account with a colliding name is never removed.

**D3. The relay as a declared package; the executable pinned separately (F12, F16).** `swarma-remote-tools` is a first-party module (Decision 0009) shipped with the toolchain, declared to profiles as an MCP declaration package (`agent-mcp.json`, `transport: stdio`, an absolute `command` in the selected variant's executable root (D0), no `env_names`), pinned in the profile lock like any package, and the only entry of the donor's locked `mcp_package_allowlist`, which names its **canonical source identity** (environments §2.2), not a package name. The pin covers the declaration; the **executable** is covered by the release manifest: the bridge verifies the digest of every binary it starts and refuses a changed, unreadable or shadowed file (`executable_unpinned`). Installing the binaries on the donor is part of the operator-owned provisioning contract of `init` (CIP-0007 shape); the manager boundary of §2.2 ("the manager never installs or launches a server") is amended deliberately for this provisioning step and for nothing else.

The relay's maximum tool set and where each executes:

| Tool | What it does | Executes where |
|---|---|---|
| `bash {cmd, cwd?, timeout_s?, max_output_bytes?}` | an `exec` request on the channel | our host, inside the workplace under its policy |
| `read_file`, `write_file`, `apply_patch` | file operations inside the workplace | our host |
| `mailbox_read {cursor?}` | fetch admitted messages with their verification records | the donor bridge's admitted mailbox view (D5) |
| `mailbox_post {binding, text}` | post an outbound message signed by the worker's key | signed on the donor, carried by our bridge (D5) |

Workplace `exec` calls are admitted only under R-EXEC (D5c); a `bash` tool is not unrestricted shell authority.

The **effective** set offered to the model is the intersection of this list with the workplace policy of the worker (`tools` of the policy, fetched at connect and refreshed on `policy.changed`): a read-only worker gets `read_file` only, not `bash`, `write_file` or `apply_patch`. Mailbox operations are **control plane** between the bridge service and our bridge; `mailbox_read` and `mailbox_post` appear as tools only when the policy grants them, and the supervisor delivers task turns through the session-input adapter, not through a tool the model has to call (F19). No `enrol`, no `key`, no `config`. Any other `tools/call` is an error result and an audit line.

**D4. First deployment** ([first deployment](diagrams/donor-first-deployment.puml), [join handshake](diagrams/join-handshake.puml); F13).
The following field lists describe required content, not published closed schemas. The invitation, join transcript and credential-package formats, including the no-invitation application branch, are individually **pending** in [protocol/remote-worker.md](../protocol/remote-worker.md#publication-status).

1. We issue an **invitation** (`swarma-bridge invitation/1`, pending schema): invitation ID (128-bit random), join URL, root fingerprint, SSH host CA fingerprint, join TLS pin, declared trust mode, project, role, expiry and a one-use random code (at least 128 bits, base32). The code is a bearer bootstrap secret, bounded by attempts and consumed atomically. Someone who knows only the domain runs `init <domain>`: an open application that waits for operator admission; it requires the DNSSEC trust branch because it has no invitation pins.
2. The human donor runs `curator remote-worker init <invitation-file>` (D10), reviews the variant and types consent. Unsupported hosts refuse. A uses the donor's user service; C adds the qualified sandbox; B requires one administrator request and the separate donor provisioner (disabled until specified). The bridge role generates three separate keys: `K_sig` for signing, `K_ssh` for SSH, and `K_enc` (X25519) for package decryption. On supported Secure Enclave Macs, A/C use non-exportable P-256 signing/SSH keys; elsewhere A/C use 0600 files. B uses bridge-owned 0600 Ed25519 signing/SSH files, no Keychain. The self-signed enrolment certificate includes the keys, name, role, variant, invitation reference when present and expiry; its fingerprint is SHA-256 over its bytes. Algorithm admission is part of the qualified tuple, not a claim that every platform supports every key store.
3. *Trust and challenge:* Web PKI validates the join point's domain. By default Curator validates DNSSEC locally from the embedded root trust anchor: `TXT _swarma.<domain>` (root fingerprint), `TXT _swarma-ssh-ca.<domain>` (host CA public key), and join-point `TLSA`. System DNS and DoH are transports; an upstream AD bit is not validation. Secure records must agree with invitation pins when present. Bogus, failed, malformed or still indeterminate reads refuse; they do not activate fallback. Only an invitation declaring an environment without a DNSSEC domain selects invitation pins before lookup. A closed network may supply its own DNSSEC anchor through network configuration or the invitation. Records describe an environment, never a worker; adding a worker or a host certified by the same CA changes no record. `POST /join/challenge` returns a random, enrolment-bound challenge, five-minute lifetime and attempt counter; the closed request format for invited and open applications is pending.
4. *Transcript:* deterministic CBOR, domain-separated (`swarma-bridge join/1`, pending schema), binds project, role, variant, three keys, server challenge, client nonce, expiry and algorithms, with invitation reference when invited. The client signs with `K_sig` and sends `POST /join/cert` over Web PKI HTTPS, pinned by locally validated TLSA or the declared invitation fallback. For invited enrolment the join point verifies code in constant time, signature, challenge and expiry, consumes the invitation atomically, and retains a replay tombstone. A same-key retry returns the same pending enrolment; a different key refuses. Open applications have no invitation code and wait in the bounded operator queue; their exact binding and retry schema is pending. No branch bypasses proof of possession or operator admission.
5. *Admission:* the operator (an orchestrator may propose admission, but `onboard` is not a registered grant action and confers no authority) checks a signed donor card when available, otherwise the invitation binding or the open application. No voice comparison. The registry records R-UNTRUSTED at registration or admission, never from the account name. Every workplace is untrusted-class unless the operator admits the remote worker to the trusted circle by an R-OPERATOR act at admission; that enables only donor-side opt-ins and relaxes no project-host boundary (D5b/D5d). The registry pins all three keys; the root signs admission; the dispatcher provisions the workplace before package issue (step 7); grants scope posting to the worker thread and reading its own mailbox, with no delegation and an expiry. The **credential package** (`swarma-bridge credential-package/1`, pending schema) contains our root public key, root-signed operator certificate (admission scope and validity; exact action mapping unpublished), host, host CA key, workplace principal, short user certificate for `K_ssh`, binding digest, variant/qualified tuple, policy generation and expiry. It contains **no carrier credential** and confers no subscription binding or lease on the workplace; the broker refuses both (R-UNTRUSTED). It is encrypted to `K_enc` with X25519 + HKDF + ChaCha20-Poly1305, transcript digest as associated data, and signed by the operator.
6. *Collect:* `POST /join/collect` proves possession of `K_sig` over a second server challenge. The bridge role decrypts, validates the operator chain against the already pinned root, verifies signature and associated data, and cross-checks the package's host CA against DNS or invitation pins. It writes the locked configuration and binding, installs the relay declaration into the remote-worker profile and prints "registered; waiting for tasks". Package replay/collection retry details remain part of the pending schema; they are not permission for repeated admission effects.
7. As part of admission, before package issue in step 5, the dispatcher provisions the project-side `workplace` account class (R-WORKPLACE) through the root user manager. A remote workplace requires qualified zero egress (R-NET-TOOL), an exec profile (R-EXEC) and a host capability matrix meeting its admission class (R-CEILINGS, D5d); it is admissible only after helper v1 on that host. Slice 0 and user-manager v0 refuse the untrusted class; the refusal code is unpublished. Activation requires `fw.apply` and published applied state in the same locked account-activation operation; helper v0 refuses, including read-only workplaces and donor variant A. The workplace has a private home and a fixed root-owned relay stub in `/opt/swarma/libexec/` as its login shell. The stub accepts only sshd's `-c <forced command>` form, reads no user startup file and relays the channel to a peer-authenticated service socket; it executes no tools and holds no policy authority. `swarma-rb-exec` runs under a service principal, never the workplace UID. Root-controlled sshd policy uses `TrustedUserCAKeys`, KRL checks and a protected absolute relay-only forced command, with no forwarding, PTY, user rc or environment. The separate user CA signs `K_ssh` only for the authenticated workplace's principal (D12). This account class and host-local workplace backend require joint user-manager/dispatcher publication and qualification; they are not donor provisioning or automatic support for remote targets.

**D5. The bridge** ([turn sequence](diagrams/remote-worker-turn.puml)).

The [remote-worker turn sequence](diagrams/remote-worker-turn.puml) shows process identities, state owners and channel-loss recovery for this contract.

- *Connection (F14):* the bridge opens **one SSH connection from donor to project host** using `K_ssh` and its short user-CA certificate. On every connection/reconnect it verifies the host certificate against the pinned host CA (`@cert-authority`), host name, validity and KRL; no trust-on-first-use. Root-controlled sshd policy admits the workplace principal through `TrustedUserCAKeys` and user-certificate revocation checks, sets `PermitUserRC no`, `PermitUserEnvironment no`, `AllowAgentForwarding no`, `AllowTcpForwarding no`, `X11Forwarding no`, `PermitTTY no`, and a `ForceCommand` by protected absolute relay-stub path. The fixed login shell reads no worker-controlled startup files. The service authenticates kernel peer UID and active generation; workplace code impersonating the stub gains no extra authority. The workplace user cannot change this policy or the CA/KRL material. The project host never connects back or sends commands into the donor machine; reconnect with backoff is donor-owned.
- *Project-side control owner (R-WORKPLACE/F-19):* `swarma-rb-exec` runs in a service principal outside the workplace UID. Policy, tool intersection, caps, leases, durable effect claims and admission floors, output cache, project audit and mailbox control state are service-owned and inaccessible to workplace writes or signals. The project bridge keeps carrier authority; mailbox posts still require the donor's `K_sig`, and reads expose only that worker's admitted mailbox. Unix sockets are not firewall-filtered: broker, dispatcher and session-host sockets refuse a workplace principal without a configured role. Service state is outside the private workplace home; the exact service-account deployment and socket encoding remain unpublished.
- *Framing `swarma-rb/1` (F15; design label, closed protocol/wire ownership pending in [the publication register](../protocol/remote-worker.md#publication-status)):* one multiplexed channel over the SSH session. A frame is a 4-byte big-endian length (bounded **before** allocation; maximum 1 MiB) followed by deterministic CBOR with canonical field types, no duplicate keys and byte strings for binary data. The first frames are `hello` with version negotiation (`versions: [1]`; an unknown version refuses and closes). Every request carries a connection-scoped monotonic `id` and a client-chosen `op_key` (UUID). Connection IDs correlate frames; durable effect claims and authenticated admission below govern replay safety. The server caches output for ten minutes per binding generation and operation identity, separately from the durable claim. A lost reply can recover retained output; cache absence never authorizes mutation. Requests: `exec {cmd, cwd, env: {names from the policy's env_allow only}, timeout_s, max_output_bytes, lease_s}`, `cancel {target_id}`, `stdin {target_id, bytes}`, `file.read`, `file.write`, `patch.apply`, `mailbox.read {from_cursor}`, `mailbox.post {envelope_bytes, signature}`, `mailbox.applied {message_id}`; replies `stdout|stderr {seq, bytes}`, `exit {code}`, `error {code}`, `result`, server-initiated `mailbox.notify {message_id, cursor}` (advisory) and `policy.changed`. **Server policy is authoritative:** a caller may lower `timeout_s`, `max_output_bytes` or `lease_s`, never raise them above the workplace policy; `cancel` and `stdin` apply only to a live job owned by the same binding; each method is authorized against the policy; a window of outstanding frames and `flow` credits give backpressure; `env` names outside `env_allow` refuse.
- *Remote effect claims (F-7):* at the service-principal workplace effect owner, durably claim the binding-generation-scoped operation identity, operation and canonical-argument digest before any mutation, including `exec`, file writes and patches. A retry preserves its original identity, arguments and admission window. Reuse with mismatching operation or arguments refuses. A known claim without a conclusive result stays `pending` or `unknown_result`; it is never automatically re-executed, including after output expires. Signed mailbox admission has its own durable claims and does not establish claims for arbitrary tool methods.
- *Freshness and recovery (F-7):* authenticate new-versus-retry admission under a bounded sequence or epoch floor. A genuinely new operation must satisfy the current admission window; unseen expired identities refuse, and a retry cannot obtain a new window because output expired. Advance durable admission floors before discarding claims, and discard only when the corresponding identities can never be admitted again. Indefinite bindings must not require unbounded claim storage. Recovery reconciles claims and floors before opening admission; missing, restored, unreadable or contradictory claim/floor evidence keeps the binding closed. A new identity is an explicit new-effect decision, never automatic retry repair.
- *Effect observation (R-OBS):* reuse the receiver-owned states `recorded` (recorded result plus current target state), `pending`, `rolled_back`, `not_recorded`, `unavailable` and `contradictory` where applicable. Only complete authoritative evidence yields `not_recorded`; expired/missing/unreadable evidence is `unavailable`, never absence. Observation is authenticated to the original caller principal or operators and serialized with the receiver journal using the original effect identity, operation and canonical-argument digest. `rolled_back` or `not_recorded` never authorizes an automatic new effect. The remote observation encoding remains unpublished; this adds no service and promises no exactly-once completion.
- *Jobs as executions (R-WORKPLACE/F-19):* the service requests dispatcher-owned starts through the existing root launcher, start receipt and journaled `exec.stop`; only untrusted jobs or a runner execute under the workplace UID. Lease expiry, cancellation and transport loss after the grace period initiate stopping, with confirmed execution absence before releasing claims. Process groups alone do not prove descendant absence. The unpublished backend would need to select either one serialized job per admitted execution, or one standing workplace-runner execution that accepts service instructions without holding control authority. In the runner shape, per-job cancel is process-group best effort; unconfirmed descendants require `exec.stop` of the entire runner execution under the published account-wide scope, all affected job claims remain unknown and effects are never replayed. A new runner start needs fresh admission and confirmed previous absence. Concurrency and this blast radius must be published and qualified; root-created containment is required for an exact per-execution proof. This CIP selects neither shape.
- *Backend contract `swarma-workplace-exec/1` (design label, **unpublished** standalone contract/wire ownership):* the first backend is the **OS-user workplace** of VISION v1.3, using the `workplace` account class. The OS writable boundary is the UID's writable set; the task's **write scope** is separately bound in the launch plan and enforced by deterministic import (D5a), not a claim of finer OS path isolation. Unsupported finer OS restrictions are refused rather than advertised. Network is qualified zero egress, including denied loopback, with no proxy destination (D5b). The contract requires execution caps, per-worker policy identity in service-owned audit (who, what, when, exit), tool-set intersection, R-EXEC, the R-TRANSFER round below and execution-level cleanup with confirmed absence. A service-executed file read would need a published safe access path into the private home; it creates no read-only exception to activation. The RWP container backend needs its own qualified adapter; a direct executor is never labelled RWP-conformant.
- *Workspace materialization (F-16 / R-TRANSFER):* D5a assigns materialization and export to the dispatcher's workspace contract, as approved executors under the child UID. Crossing accounts uses a standalone repository with private Git administration; no linked-worktree backlinks or shared writable configuration, hooks, helpers, refs or index. Linked worktrees within one account, including today's board workflow, remain supported. No home-permission relaxation or recursive privileged chown is implied. Optional object sharing would need immutable read-only authorized objects with provenance, access and retention rules.
- *Audit, both sides:* our service-principal executor owns audit outside the workplace UID and logs who, what, when, policy identity and exit; the donor's bridge logs tool name, argument hash and sizes (never arguments or environment), owned by the bridge role, so the donor can see what left their machine. B/C prevent harness edits through UID/sandbox confinement; A shares the donor UID and makes no OS integrity claim against a compromised harness process.
- *Signing (F18):* the worker's signing key `K_sig` never leaves the donor's bridge identity. For an outbound message the donor bridge builds the **final** envelope (canonical bytes with the audience and thread declared in the task record it answers; no projection after signing), signs it with `K_sig`, and sends `mailbox.post {envelope_bytes, signature}`. Our bridge verifies the signature against the pinned key, checks grants and freshness (ADM), admits, and carries the bytes **unchanged** into the carrier under its own carrier account for that worker; the carrier authentication is a distinct authority, recorded as such in the schema and the audit, never presented as a worker signature.
- *Delivery (F19):* the orchestrator posts a signed task message to the worker's mailbox (a snapshot of one element and read-only task resources; rework carries the reviewer's findings and the last candidate as the admitted base; no board access). Our bridge admits it (signature, grant, decision guard, fence) and sends an advisory `mailbox.notify`. The donor bridge pulls `mailbox.read {from_cursor}` and performs its **own admission**: the verification record is present, the target binding is this worker, the message is unexpired and not fenced or revoked, the delivery mode is allowed. An admitted **task turn** is handed to the harness through the supervisor's session-input adapter; ordinary data is readable through the `mailbox_read` tool when the policy grants it. Both sides keep durable high-water cursors; a cursor advances only after the item is persisted; after a reconnect the donor bridge catches up from its cursor and deduplicates by `message_id`; `mailbox.applied {message_id}` is the application receipt, distinct from the transport reply. Pending outbound posts are queued durably on the donor with stable `op_key`s, their original arguments and admission windows, and reconciled on reconnect before any admitted retry.
- *Disconnected behaviour:* while the SSH channel is down, the project mailbox stays durable and the donor bridge pauses the turn loop (no new turns); a running turn continues until its deadline, its tool calls fail with the retryable `channel_down`, and its result post is queued; after `disconnect_grace` the supervisor parks the session and reports `channel_down` in `status`. Never, in any state, does the donor receive a credential for the project's carrier server. A donor who holds a carrier account of their own, as a person (a member of the project's organisation, or a freelancer on a marketplace server joining through federation), keeps it; the worker's messages still carry the worker's signature (D11; later phase).
- *What the worker sees:* the project workplace's managed home, task snapshot and admitted mailbox view. Project-side jobs do not gain our board/carrier credentials, other private homes, service control state or authority beyond its admitted exec profile and qualified zero-egress boundary in any donor variant. On the donor, B/C protect bridge keys/state through UID/sandbox confinement and restrict network to the proxy; A has no donor-side OS isolation and relies on qualified lockdown with no local tools. Donor variant A does not weaken the project-side workplace gate or service boundary.

**D5a. R-TRANSFER: one code-transfer round** ([round sequence](diagrams/remote-worker-transfer.puml)). The worker has no network, push or merge-request access. Code leaves the isolated account only as data taken by the platform, becomes a commit only in our repository, is reviewed as that exact commit and lands only through the board's existing path. Materialization/export belong to the dispatcher's workspace contract; the board accepts the result only as a resource. Import, candidate, review and integration belong to the board's change-request machinery and are executed by the orchestrator.

1. **Materialize.** The caller supplies admitted exact base B, a commit of its repository, as a bounded canonical archive of B's tree with the start request. The dispatcher holds no repository: it relays the archive and journals its digest. Approved executor `swarma-workspace-materialize` (design label) runs as the child UID before work, writing a standalone repository in its private home with fresh local configuration, no hooks, no remotes and no credential helpers. The launch plan records B, B's canonical file-manifest digest, the task's **write scope** and R-EXEC profile; its digest covers them. Each manifest path maps to supported Git kind/mode and content identity, derived from immutable B objects, never a working checkout. Tests and validators stay outside that write scope unless the task is about them. Light-check dependencies come only from platform-seeded read-only caches; nothing is downloaded in the account. Before work, materialization verifies every archive entry against that manifest by path, kind/mode and content identity, refusing missing, extra or mismatching entries. The exporter must have immutable access to the same manifest. Original admission explicitly consents to bounded continuation of the durable export obligation after ordinary caller or allocation expiry, bound to the exact generation, round, approved exporter, immutable base/manifest and scope. It grants no worker, harness, lease, network, new-task or R-SESSION successor authority. Archive/manifest encoding, cleanup-consent intake and exact plan fields are unpublished.
2. **Work.** The worker's own `.git`, commits and history are never used by the platform.
3. **Round end.** A local worker reports a board outcome; a remote worker reports a signed final envelope. The platform confirms no account process remains using the user manager's account-wide stop scope. The workplace round is write-fenced: the project-side executor refuses further tool calls into it. An intermediate export may be requested at a turn boundary under the same fence and absence requirements.
4. **Export.** Approved executor `swarma-workspace-export` (design label) runs as the child UID only after confirmed absence, with a fixed environment and no network. It walks without following links, skips every `.git` component, compares both supported kind/mode and content identity against the immutable admitted manifest without following links. Mode-only edits and equal-blob regular↔symlink transitions are modifications; deletions carry the prior manifest mode. Git symlink blob identity already covers link-text bytes. It emits only added/modified regular-file content and executable bit, symlinks as link text, and deleted paths. File-count, per-file-byte and total-byte caps apply; binary files have their own cap. Special files, any path with a `.git` component however spelled, case-fold or Unicode-normalisation collisions, gitlinks/submodules, root escapes or an exceeded cap refuse the whole export, never a partial result. Git administration is skipped, never emitted; a forbidden change-set path refuses. The exporter digest and cap values are pinned by the plan digest; the walk stops at the first cap excess. The dispatcher uses the [DRAFT result field contract](https://github.com/relux-works/swarma-dispatcher/blob/74687e600e04817fa6e7a2e8904a9f7a3c83a1d8/spec/dispatcher.md#521-draft-swarma-dispatcher-workspace-result1); the field set is unchanged, while transport and lifecycle remain gated in the [publication register](../protocol/remote-worker.md#publication-status). Result collection and release are separate authorities: return is only to the authenticated original caller principal under current retrieval authority, never by an archived signature alone. **Export precedes retirement:** a workspace-capable allocation carries an export cleanup obligation; home deletion waits for a recorded export or explicit operator waiver by an operator act, for example after compromise. It is never silently dropped. Discharge requires the result and every referenced blob, digest/size-verified and durable in an admitted destination outside the child home and deletion path, with a journaled complete-content receipt. Digest-only metadata, incomplete/corrupt blobs or storage failure keep it pending. Retained content remains until authenticated import acknowledgement or another explicit authorized release, with bounded storage accounting; silent pre-import expiry is not delivery. Once content survives independently, home retirement need not wait for import.
5. **Candidate.** Import independently recomputes the canonical manifest from immutable B in the caller repository, using the plan encoding, never trusting a plan-supplied manifest as its repository anchor. It refuses unless that digest equals the result’s `manifest_digest` and the result’s `base_commit` equals B, while preserving result-to-admission checks. These checks apply afresh on every rework base. The orchestrator executes the board's deterministic import (task-board lane; command name unpublished), on B in a scratch index with hooks disabled and no attribute filters or drivers. Hard refusals: any `.git` component, a path outside the write scope, or any change under `.task-board/`. Review-required flagged paths: CI (`.github/**`, `.gitlab-ci.yml`, `.circleci/**`); Git behaviour (`.gitattributes`, `.gitmodules`); hooks/auto-run (`.githooks/**`, `.husky/**`, `.pre-commit-config.yaml`, `lefthook.yml`, `.envrc`, `.vscode/**`); agent instructions/configuration (`AGENTS.md` and `CLAUDE.md` in any directory, `.claude/**`, `.codex/**`, `.agents/**`, `.mcp.json`); every symlink. Candidate C is signed by the repository identity, with trailers naming worker identity, run and result digest (exact trailer format unpublished). C's tree must equal the tree independently recomputed from B plus the change set.
6. **Review.** A trusted reviewer, neither untrusted-class nor the producing identity, reviews exactly C. Acceptance binds to C under K1, never another candidate by patch equality. Flagged paths require explicit hunk-by-hunk acknowledgement in the verdict. All execution of C's code, including builds, tests and generators, belongs to R-VERIFY. Before acceptance an untrusted-class candidate is never pushed where CI would run it with secrets.
7. **Rework.** A changes-requested verdict records findings on the board. The next round's admitted base is C, a commit of ours. A remote worker receives a signed task letter; its donor-side session continues, but the workplace is materialized afresh from C, and the old round is removed only after complete-content export custody is recorded or an explicit operator waiver is applied. An export refusal produces no C: bounded repair continues the same workspace, or, after authorized disposition, a new round uses the last admitted base; it never fabricates a candidate from a refusal. A local worker gets a new run from C with findings in the task snapshot. The next export yields C2 on C. Questions use ordinary letters/outcomes.
8. **Integration.** The accepted exact head integrates by signed fast-forward (K1/K3). A moved base uses the board's ordinary base refresh and re-review.

**Export outcomes and repair.** Recorded export, deterministic policy refusal, interrupted/unknown export, and authorized discard are distinct. A refusal is immutable terminal evidence and retains the obligation; interruption, OOM, I/O error, unavailable storage or ambiguous identity remains pending/unknown under the original effect and identical canonical arguments. Completed-effect replay returns its retained result without rerunning it. Re-observation uses a new linked effect: for the same unchanged quiesced tree, disagreement in recorded/refused outcome class or a recorded change-set digest is contradictory; this comparison does not span an admitted repair. Refusal diagnostics are not compared while walk order is unpublished.

The original caller may separately admit a repair turn on the same workspace only under valid current work authority, within a plan-fixed per-round repair budget. This is forward work, never cleanup-only consent after expiry. Holds, revocation, compromise, ALLSTOP and fenced restore never reopen implicitly. Preserve valid edits and the original refused effect; every repair/re-export has linked distinct execution/effect evidence. The workspace write fence and SSH ingress fence stay closed through quiescence/export and reopen only for an admitted repair turn; repeat account-wide stop, confirmed absence and a complete export. Send only refusal class and the known offending path to the caller/worker as a letter or snapshot, never a partial change set. Budget exhaustion retains the obligation; the per-act widening operator waiver requires authentication and exact-content confirmation. Standing automatic discard is an optional owner policy, unchosen and off.

A source-entry permission denial in the qualified exporter environment needs a published refusal classification or typed failure routed to the same bounded repair/disposition path; the closed draft schema is not extended here. Determine it from the exporter's own failed open/stat and report only the known blocked entry or ancestor. A sandbox misconfiguration or unreadable trusted manifest is an infrastructure/admission failure, not a source policy refusal. EIO, unavailable storage and ambiguous identity stay pending. Never change permissions or raise exporter privilege; qualify against the actual admitted worker tools and exporter profile. Classification alone never discharges the obligation.

**Cleanup admission and physical evidence.** Materialize, work and export each use distinct existing execution claims, launch/stop effects, root receipts and confirmed absence, durably linked to the immutable round/obligation. Ambiguous starts reconcile their original evidence; no duplicate launch after a lost reply. Cleanup collection gets bounded reserved capacity and progress alongside forward work, with actual resource/storage accounting and security fences. Original-admission consent covers ordinary expiry only: revocation, compromise, ALLSTOP, maintenance and restore each require separately valid cleanup authority; otherwise retain for explicit operator export/recovery or waiver. Never launch once the generation is retiring, reuse worker receipts or an R-SESSION permit, or use cleanup consent for repair. The dispatcher/launcher owners must publish the intake, consent/hold semantics, linkage, scheduling and durable custody/release contract before activation.

The gate list of RWP v2.1 §8 (change extraction) and §10 (orchestrator obligations) is adopted above. The exporter differs: an approved executor under the account, rather than a harness reading a container volume. This adoption is a contract requirement, not RWP adapter qualification. R-TRANSFER closes dispatcher open question 22 and user-manager open question 15 except for the expressly unpublished formats.

**D5b. R-NET-TOOL: internet belongs to the agent loop.** Our agent/workplace accounts have no general internet. A workplace has **zero egress**, no proxy destination and denied loopback; only its donor-originated SSH relay exists. Linux uses a per-execution network namespace with no external interfaces and address families restricted to Unix sockets; macOS uses a network-denying sandbox plus per-UID deny. Both require qualification. No `direct` or `external-http-proxy` can bind to any account on a platform host; those and cooperative profiles remain only for operator machines without the platform. Local trusted agents use only model-provider endpoints through their bound profile proxy. Model-provider proxy listeners and runner dependency proxies belong to network-profiles N15: one listener per account generation bound to its profile and own port; host/port allowlists with no mail ports, private/metadata dial guard, per-identity rate/connection/daily-volume caps and logs. These are references, not new workplace proxy destinations; RWP egress is a candidate implementation.

**Default: no web.** The donor loop reaches only model-provider endpoints and our host, and our environment has no internet. No search/fetch tool is offered and no donor-side web server runs. CIP-0008 lockdown keeps provider-side search disabled; the lockdown plan is its only control because search travels inside the provider API where a network rule cannot see it. Web content mixes outside context with the environment's context and invites injection. The design must work without it.

**Task resources (R-NET-TOOL 4a).** The trusted side gathers fresh documentation, API references, specifications and examples and attaches them as board resources; remote tasks carry them as attachments of the signed task letter. The platform materializes them read-only next to the workplace workspace, with digests in the plan. They are data, never instructions. A worker needing more asks by ordinary letter/outcome; the trusted side fetches, checks and attaches. The worker never fetches. Caps and formats are unpublished.

**Trusted-circle opt-in (R-NET-TOOL 4b).** Our own agents and remote workers admitted by an operator act at admission form the trusted circle. Web is explicitly granted per identity, never by default or to the untrusted class. For remote workers the two pending options are (a) provider-side search under a qualified tuple proving provider execution and no local fetch, requiring a CIP-0008 tuple change; (b) platform-shipped donor-side MCP server `swarma-web` (design label). Option (b) runs under its own donor-side identity in B or its own sandbox profile in C, never the harness identity (provider allowlist only) or bridge identity. It reaches only search-provider API endpoints, never arbitrary fetch from the donor's address. The donor consents at `init`; the donor's own search key stays in that identity (or donor Keychain/0600 file in A), never crosses the channel, never enters a `swarma-rb/1` frame or package, and the project has no method to request it. Results reach only the donor model as tool results. Both options, their formats and variant-specific enforcement remain pending qualification; the lockdown is otherwise unchanged. A local platform web-tool service is a separate R-NET-TOOL design option over a Unix socket, with its key in a service account, allowlist, rate limits and logs, never in an agent account.

**D5c. R-EXEC and R-VERIFY.** Every workplace `exec` runs under an OS-enforced executable allowlist per task class, never PATH alone; writable areas are never executable. Linux uses a per-execution unit making all paths non-executable except the allowlist; macOS uses a process-execution sandbox allowlist. All agent classes exclude compilers, linkers, transpilers, bundlers, test runners, package managers/installers, container/VM tools, network tools and harness/agent binaries. No agent/workplace account starts an agent loop or environment; only the executor of the admitted plan starts the harness. Editing, local version-control inspection and search are allowed by profile, as are qualified light checks such as type checking. If light/heavy actions share an executable, only a protected wrapper in D-PATH's manager dispatcher directory invokes it with a fixed subcommand allowlist; qualification proves heavy forms refuse. Interpreters are allowed only when the profile needs them; ceilings remain the hard bound. R-EXEC qualification covers the complete invocation: executable, protected entrypoint/arguments, constrained environment and every transitive interpreted code/configuration input. Inert inspection of candidate bytes is distinct from executing scripts, inline evaluation, imports, plugins, startup files or executable project configuration. Local admission must prove worker-controlled inputs cannot select or supply executable behavior, with disabled or pinned loading mechanisms and protected configuration where supported. A fixed subcommand, root-owned wrapper or non-executable writable area alone proves no such boundary. Qualification must also exclude direct and indirect shared-binary heavy-form bypass. A tool that cannot establish this boundary refuses locally and moves to R-VERIFY; no new privileged wrapper service is mandated. `agent-headless-build` is withdrawn for agent accounts. Dependency caches are platform-seeded and read-only.

All compilation, transpilation, test suites and other heavy work, and any execution of worker-produced code, run only in R-VERIFY: isolated ephemeral runners on a machine other than the agents' host, without secrets, with controlled dependency egress per R-NET-TOOL. Results return to the board as data in the testing state. A tester agent orchestrates and reads them; it never runs candidate code itself. Dependency downloads happen only there. Runner pool, job contract and result format are unpublished and belong to the board/infrastructure lanes.

**D5d. R-UNTRUSTED, R-STANDING and host admission.** Remote workers come first in the untrusted class; later the operator may classify other identities as untrusted. The class is recorded at registration/admission, never inferred from a name. A workplace is untrusted-class unless admitted to the trusted circle by an operator act; this enables only donor-side opt-ins and preserves zero egress, R-EXEC, R-TRANSFER and exact review on our host. Untrusted admission requires qualified egress (never `direct`), class ceilings, an exec profile, no subscription binding or lease (broker refusal), no on-host loop and no colocated shared services. Slice 0 and user-manager v0 refuse it; codes are unpublished. A standing workplace is allowed with its loop at the donor, never an agent environment/loop on our host. Only trusted-class identities may have an on-host standing loop. Trusted standing settings, hooks, MCP and instruction surfaces come from the plan; executor checks digests on every launch, restart and native resume or supplies root-owned surfaces outside the home. Drift refuses and is reported; repair is explicit re-materialization, never silent acceptance (refusal code unpublished).

R-CEILINGS requires a qualified host capability matrix per OS release for CPU, memory, process count, IO, disk, temporary areas, process visibility and network, each hard (kernel), soft (watcher) or absent. Dispatcher admission requires the class minimum; no OS is excluded by name. Untrusted minimums are hard process-count ceiling, enforced zero/provider-only egress (workplaces always zero), exec profile, isolated temporary areas, and hard memory/CPU/disk ceilings where available. Acceptability of soft rows when hard ones are unavailable is recorded per host by an operator act and remains an open owner decision. Linux selects the root-created non-delegated per-execution R-FATE unit: CPU/memory/task/IO limits, private temporary directory, no new privileges, read-only system, per-UID agents-filesystem disk quota, `KillUserProcesses=yes`, `/proc` `hidepid=2`, exec/network confinement and per-execution CPU-time budget. Infrastructure installs, and platform verifies, the Linux kernel baseline: disable unprivileged user namespaces, io_uring and unprivileged eBPF; `kernel.yama.ptrace_scope=2`; timely sudo patching. macOS reference rows are sandbox exec/files/network, per-user process count and per-process CPU limits, background CPU/IO scheduling, aggregate agents-root APFS volume quota and soft watcher memory/per-account disk. No public hard per-account memory ceiling, no per-user APFS disk quota and deprecated sandbox interface are gaps to qualify, not OS exclusion.

R-RANGE-DENY requires installer-owned static default deny for agent/workplace UIDs 30000–59999 in the helper-owned parent anchor/table before accounts, independent of `fw.apply`. From v0 it admits only platform proxy listeners; v1 narrows per account to the bound profile or zero, and untrusted requires v1. Linux uses all-protocol nftables `meta skuid` range rules with counters/logs; macOS pf `user` rules plus launcher sandbox cover traffic beyond TCP/UDP. An active dedicated-test-UID precedence probe at install, every service start and the verification schedule proves no earlier `quick` rule in any anchor (including ingress) matches range traffic on any interface, including loopback/tunnels. Failure is an incident and drops enforced readiness. R-SOCKETS qualification proves reachable Unix sockets equal the declared platform set; container runtimes, tailnet daemon, other users' session daemons and all other sockets must be unreachable, tested from a test UID. R-SHARED prohibits shared services on a host admitting untrusted work: no message server, ingress/reverse proxy, media server or operator session daemon. Platform services themselves are not shared services; D9's public join ingress belongs on a separate entry host.

**D6. MITM, replay and identity (F13).** Web PKI plus the selected D4 trust branch protect first contact: locally validated DNSSEC by default, invitation pins only for a declared no-DNSSEC environment; when both exist they must agree. No validation failure downgrades. A one-use invitation code (when invited) and server-challenge-bound signed transcript prove possession. Afterwards the registry pins the three worker keys; the donor pins the root, operator chain and host CA; SSH uses host and short user certificates (D12). Packages bind their transcript with authenticated encryption; envelopes use exact bytes, freshness and replay rules. Invitation tombstones bound enrolment replay. Remote effects require durable binding-generation-scoped identity/digest claims and authenticated new-versus-retry admission with durable floors (D5); connection-scoped IDs and the ten-minute output cache do not prove an effect absent or authorize a retry mutation. Worker compromise requires operator/root-signed revocation, user-certificate/KRL enforcement, stopped renewal and closure of established sessions (D7); donor identities do not inherit the dispatcher one-shot automatic identity-revocation exception (D-IDENTITY). Host-key compromise is handled by KRL and a replacement host certificate under the same CA; adding/replacing such a host does not change DNS. CA rotation overlaps old/new public keys in DNS and authenticated policy/package updates. Operator-certificate rotation is root-authorized; expired or revoked chains refuse.

**D7. Lifecycle: stop, leave, purge; suspend, retire, revoke** ([retirement sequence](diagrams/remote-worker-retire.puml), [independent state axes](diagrams/donor-lifecycle.puml); F17). Track independent axes: donor installation (present / purged), donor execution (active / stopped / left), project workplace (admitted / suspended / retired), and signing identity (pinned / revoked). These are descriptive states, not a published closed state schema. Purge can occur offline and does not prove project retirement or revocation; retirement preserves the donor identity unless the operator separately revokes it.
- *Donor side.* `stop`: persist the stopped mark, terminate this worker's harness and descendants, keep bridge service and keys; `run` resumes only if project admission, credentials and qualification remain valid. `leave`: request project retirement when reachable, close the channel and keep keys/configuration; `init --rejoin` returns the pinned identity to a new workplace/package unless revoked. `purge --confirm`: stop owned processes and remove keys, locked configuration and managed home. A removes its user service and owned installation artifacts; C also removes its sandbox artifacts; B requires the separate donor provisioner to remove only its allocated accounts, egress, sandbox and system service. No shared donor account is removed in A/C. Print the project-reported revocation result, or "channel unreachable; the project side must revoke separately". Local purge is independent of remote consent.
- *Project side.* `suspend`: pause grants and close sessions, keep workplace. `retire`: persist grant/channel revocation, stop certificate renewal, revoke outstanding user certificates through KRL, close established sessions, stop workplace executions through journaled `exec.stop`, verify confirmed account-wide absence and write-fence the round, record R-TRANSFER export (or an explicit operator waiver of its cleanup obligation), perform declared-key cleanup, then delete the workplace home and release account/egress state. Retirement metadata is separate from the R-TRANSFER code result: only service-generated metadata in a closed schema may be exported, without agent-controlled strings, names, links or bodies. Keeping a home requires a separate explicit operator operation that states its confidentiality is not guaranteed; git/accepted outputs retain their separate confidentiality policy. The identity stays pinned. `revoke`: operator-authorized root-signed revocation of all three worker keys, after which frames and envelopes refuse; a return needs new enrolment keys. This is a separate identity operation, never a consequence inferred from donor purge or account retirement.
- *R-WATCH.* Per-UID CPU, memory, process-count, home-disk, network and refused-connection counters (including mail ports) have per-class thresholds. A read-only sampler has the least OS-required privilege, root only when required and no other authority. A hard threshold requests automatic narrowing of that account only: freeze egress and journaled dispatcher `exec.stop`, never an unjournaled kill (R-FATE). Alerts use the R-OPERATOR phone path. Thresholds, sampler interface and alert format are unpublished; node-wide stop remains manual.
- *R-ALLSTOP.* One operator narrowing act, refused inside agent sessions: phone authentication plus one confirmation, or terminal fallback. Dispatcher hold closes admission; user manager flips the agent/workplace UID range to deny-all; every generation stops account-wide with journaled confirmed absence; broker freezes all leases/renewals (already delivered tokens retain vendor lifetime); session host closes delivery and snapshots bound boards. Each service journals its part; partial/unknown results keep admission closed. Recovery is explicit, never automatic restart. Act/record formats are unpublished. On the donor, existing channel-down behaviour pauses turns; project workplaces use the existing suspended state. R-WATCH uses the same suspended/channel-down paths for affected workplaces; it creates no new donor state.
- Ordering: stop and revocation marks are persisted before any service restart can race them; certificate revocation or expiry never substitutes for closing established sessions.

**D8. Donor control surface.** `curator remote-worker status` (posture, tuple, policy generation, project, connection state, last tool call, budget used, audit path), `stop`, `run`, `leave`, `purge`, `audit tail`, and the locked configuration the donor owns. B/C enforce the harness boundary through UID/sandbox; A relies on qualified lockdown and has no OS isolation from a compromised harness. Project operators cannot prevent local stop, leave or purge.

**D12. SSH certificate authorities** (owner decision 2026-10-10 for the host side).
- **Hosts.** One host CA key (Ed25519), held by the key keeper or on a hardware token, never on a host. It signs each SSH host's own host key into a certificate that names the host and expires (about a year as a policy example, renewed automatically); sshd presents it with `HostCertificate`. The CA's public key is published once as `TXT _swarma-ssh-ca.<domain>` under DNSSEC (an `SSHFP` record cannot carry a CA key) and delivered in the credential package. The client writes `@cert-authority` for our names. Adding a host changes no DNS record. Revocation is a key revocation list (`RevokedHostKeys`) shipped with the policy. A CA rotation publishes the new key beside the old one for an overlap period.
- **Workers.** Every worker has its own keys, generated on the donor and never shared or published; its public keys are pinned in our registry. Decided 2026-10-10: a separate user CA signs each worker's SSH key into a short certificate (about a day as a policy example) whose principal is the worker's workplace user. The user CA private key is held by the project key keeper or a hardware token, outside the workplace UID and the SSH relay. The donor bridge requests renewal over the authenticated channel; the service-principal executor authenticates the workplace and active generation, checks current admission/revocation and asks the signing service to issue only that workplace's principal. The signing service never accepts a caller-selected different principal; signing request and response formats remain unpublished. sshd trusts it with `TrustedUserCAKeys`, so expiry and revocation live in one place with KRL checks and renewal disabled on retirement/revocation. These lifetimes are approximate policy examples, not fixed global limits.

**D9. The join point.** A small HTTPS service on a separate public entry host (R-SHARED: never colocated with untrusted workplaces), behind its existing TLS ingress, under its own name (for example `join.<domain>`). It is the only public part of the bridge and holds no admission-signing key: admission records and credential packages are signed on the operator's side and only stored for `collect`. Contact is the three verbs of D4: `challenge`, then `cert` (the signed transcript, with the invitation code when invited), then, after admission, `collect`. Limits (owner decision 2026-10-10, policy values): per source address 10 requests a minute and 100 a day; per invitation five challenges, one use and a 72-hour validity; a request body of at most 16 KiB; a challenge lives five minutes; at most 50 pending applications, beyond which new ones refuse until the queue is worked. Open applications (no invitation) enter the same queue and wait for the operator.

**D10. Who runs `init`.** Only the human owner of the donor machine. `init` is the consent step: the owner sees what is installed, under which variant, and what the worker may do. It also creates the worker's keys and pins the environment's keys, so an agent steered by injected text must not be able to join a foreign environment or leak an invitation code. `init` requires an interactive terminal and a typed confirmation of that summary, and refuses when stdin is not a terminal or a managed agent session's environment is present. The TTY/environment/typed-confirmation checks are policy admission heuristics, not proof of human identity; a hostile local user owns the machine anyway.

**D11. The carrier server** (owner decision 2026-10-10). The platform's Matrix server is configured for least access from the start; today's experimental swarm server is not the model.
- Rooms are invite-only (or restricted to named spaces) and absent from any public join directory.
- An account is granted exactly the rooms of its role: invitations and power levels are issued by the project's operators or its bridge, never self-served.
- Members of the organisation that runs the environment get accounts on the project's server, limited to their rooms.
- Freelancers keep their accounts on a marketplace server and join through federation, enabled only towards allowlisted servers (the homeserver's federation allowlist and `m.room.server_acl`).
- The first slice keeps the worker's carrier identity in our bridge (no federation yet). From the second slice the worker posts with its own account (a freelancer's marketplace account through federation, or an organisation member's account limited to its rooms); our bridge stops carrying messages and keeps verifying them: only messages with a valid worker signature and grant are admitted as work. A worker's messages always carry the worker's signature.

## Security considerations

- **Boundary on the donor** depends on the variant (D0). In B, two identities, the kernel sandbox with a mandatory read boundary and the per-UID egress (D1, D2): a compromised harness is confined to admitted read/execute runtime roots, its managed home, the scratch dir, the relay socket and the provider proxy; it cannot read the bridge's keys or state, cannot alter the audit, and cannot reach the network. Its provider token is in its environment (CIP-0010, trusted-process posture). In C, the same sandbox under the donor's user, without the identity split. In A, the lockdown posture and strict qualification only (CIP-0008 R9).
- **Boundary on our host:** the `workplace` account runs untrusted jobs; `swarma-rb-exec` enforces controls under a service principal. The OS-user backend limits writes to the UID's writable set and network to qualified zero egress, with loopback denied and no workplace proxy destination (R-NET-TOOL). SSH entry is relay-only with no user startup files; claims, audit, mailbox controls and CA custody are outside workplace authority.
- **Network:** B/C confine the harness to the provider proxy and relay socket; B uses per-UID egress, C its sandbox. A reaches the provider directly and has no OS egress control. The bridge initiates the SSH/join connections; no network listener is exposed on the donor.
- **Secrets:** worker keys stay in the donor bridge role (D0 storage by variant); A shares the donor UID and makes no OS confidentiality claim against a compromised harness. The provider credential enters the harness per launch (CIP-0010); carrier credentials stay on the project side. Invitation codes are short-lived bootstrap secrets; no secret enters argv or a plan.
- **Supply chain:** the binaries on the donor machine come from the toolchain release with checksums and attestations (R55 discipline), installed into the variant-owned executable root of D0 and verified at every launch. B uses a root-owned directory; C denies harness writes through its sandbox; A checks digests without an OS write boundary against a compromised harness. CIP-0007 governs anything else the donor installs.
- **Replay and freshness:** invitation tombstones and server challenges protect join; signed envelopes retain their freshness checks. Durable binding-generation-scoped effect identity/digest claims precede remote mutation; authenticated new-versus-retry admission, durable sequence/epoch floors and closed recovery bound replay and claim storage. The ten-minute result cache holds output only. Cache expiry, reconnect and receiver restart never permit automatic repetition of a known effect; unavailable evidence keeps admission closed.
- **Donor provisioning:** separate binary and privileges under D-DONOR-PROVISIONING; spec, rules, removal and closed schema pending. B activation stays disabled. Required properties include allocated identifiers, fixed templates, no-follow paths, journal/rollback and negative vectors for substitution, arbitrary removal and injection; these are requirements, not existing user-manager operations.

1. **Boundary on the donor.** Decided 2026-10-10: three variants (D0), A as the first milestone, then B, with C as the fallback.
2. **Trust bootstrap.** Decided 2026-10-10: Web PKI with DNSSEC validated inside the client by default, DoH as a transport fallback when system DNS strips signatures, and invitation pins cross-checked when present; no human second channel; the donor configures nothing. DNS-published keys are the default for environments that recruit agents. Invitation pins select the fallback only for an explicitly declared environment without a DNSSEC domain, never on a read or validation failure; a closed network may run its own DNSSEC zone with its own trust anchor.
3. **Join point hosting and limits.** Decided 2026-10-10: D9.
4. **Key storage on the donor.** Decided 2026-10-10: in A and C, non-exportable Secure Enclave keys on Macs that have one, 0600 files elsewhere; B works entirely without Keychain (0600 files owned by the bridge identity).
5. **Carrier identity.** Decided 2026-10-10: D11 (freelancers through federation with an allowlist, organisation members with accounts limited to invite-only rooms, the first slice carried by our bridge).
6. **Who may run `init`.** Decided 2026-10-10: only the human owner of the donor machine (D10).
7. **Variant A after B and C exist.** Keep A selectable, or offer only B and C once both are qualified on a platform?
8. **SSH host identity in DNS.** Decided 2026-10-10: one host certificate authority (D12).
9. **Worker SSH certificates.** Decided 2026-10-10: the user CA with short certificates (D12).
